Most bad days on the bike start at the kitchen counter, or in a jersey pocket with nothing in it. Here's the quick version of our [fueling guide](/fuel/guide), sorted by how long you're out. It's general guidance for healthy adults. Your needs depend on you, the heat and the effort, so test it in training.

## Under an hour

Water, and a snack if you're hungry. A normal meal a few hours before is plenty. On hard efforts or races, a few sips of a carb drink can help. After, your next normal meal does the job.

## One to two and a half hours

This is where fueling starts to matter. Eat a carb-forward meal 1 to 4 hours before and start hydrated. On the bike, aim for 30 to 60 g of carbs per hour, and start in the first 30 minutes. Waiting until you're hungry is waiting too long. Drink to thirst, often around 400 to 800 ml an hour. Count carbs from everything: bottles, gels, bars, gummies, a gas station burrito. It all counts.

## Over two and a half hours, and race day

Fuel more, and practice it before race day. A bigger carb-forward meal 2 to 4 hours before, plus a small snack 30 to 60 minutes out. On the bike, up to 90 g of carbs per hour if your gut is trained for it. High rates take practice, so build up on training rides and never try it first on race day. Above about 60 g an hour, fuels that mix carb types are easier to absorb. Add electrolytes and keep sipping.

## Hot days, any length

More fluid and more sodium than usual. Start well hydrated, consider salt before you roll, and drink on a schedule, not just when you're parched. If your kit dries with white salt lines, you're probably a salty sweater. One warning: don't overdrink. Drinking a lot more than you sweat, for hours, can drop your blood sodium to dangerous levels. If you feel confused, get a bad headache, or feel sick while drinking a lot, stop and get help right away.

## After the ride

Refill the tank with carbs and protein within a couple of hours. Weigh yourself before and after. For every kilo you lose, drink roughly 1.25 to 1.5 liters over the next few hours.
