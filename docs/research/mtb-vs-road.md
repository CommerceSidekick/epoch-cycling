# How mountain biking differs from road cycling

Research brief for Epoch MTB (owner request 2026-11-16). This is training
context for copy and programming. It is not medical advice. Several of the
studies are small, so the site states trends rather than precise numbers, and
nothing on the site promises an outcome.

## Short version

Road racing rewards one engine: steady aerobic power held for hours in a fixed,
aero position. Mountain biking asks for that engine and four more things: repeated
short surges above maximal power, a working upper body and grip, technical skill,
and a body that can absorb vibration and crashes. Training for MTB therefore adds
upper body, grip, trunk and repeated-sprint work to the aerobic base road riders
already build.

## What the evidence says

| Demand | MTB | Road | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intensity pattern | Cross-country (XCO) races run about 80 minutes to 2 hours at close to 90% of max heart rate, with more than 80% of race time above lactate threshold. Power swings hard: one study counted about 334 efforts above maximal aerobic power per race, averaging about 4 seconds each. | Longer, steadier efforts with more time at low power and fewer surges. Data from road cycling only partly transfers to off-road events. | Impellizzeri and Marcora 2007; 2021 XCO race-pacing study; Lee (thesis, 2003); Journal of Sports Science and Medicine 2015 |
| Upper body | Riders hold high isometric contractions in the arms, shoulders and trunk to control the bike over terrain. Downhill riders show the highest triceps activity; XC riders more forearm (brachioradialis) activity. Simulated downhill loads the chest (pectoralis major) about as much as a wall push. | Upper body mainly holds position. | Hurst et al. 2012 (EMG, DH vs XCO); Hurst et al. (wheel size); simulated downhill EMG study |
| Grip and vibration | Grip strength drops measurably after a single downhill run (about 5.5% in one study). Vibration raises oxygen cost at the same power output. | Low vibration, smooth surfaces. | Burr et al. 2012; Kirkwood (vibration) |
| Enduro and downhill | Timed stages run above 90% of max heart rate with blood lactate near 15 mmol/L after a stage. Much of the effort comes from pumping, weighting and unweighting the bike, which heart rate under-reports. High aerobic and anaerobic power are entry tickets; skill and riding volume separate the best. | Not applicable. | Kirkwood et al. 2017; enduro review 2021 |
| Body type and engine | XC riders look like climbers: high aerobic power relative to body mass (VO2max above 70 mL/kg/min at elite level). Downhill riders have lower aerobic and anaerobic capacity than XC and road riders, and depend more on strength and skill. | Same aerobic engine; aerodynamics matter more. | Impellizzeri and Marcora 2007; Dascombe et al. (road, XC, DH, BMX) |
| Injuries | Most injuries are to the upper body: clavicle fractures first, then hand and wrist (including scaphoid). Falls from the bike cause most of them. | More overuse issues tied to long, flexed, aero posture: low back pain, linked to lumbar flexion and weak back-extensor endurance. | Bigdon et al. 2022 systematic review; MTB injury epidemiology review; US NEISS 10-year analysis; Streisfeld et al. (low back pain review) |
| Strength training | Eight weeks of trunk training improved trunk strength and bike control in female mountain bikers. Heavy strength training improves cycling economy and performance in cyclists generally. | Heavy strength work raised peak power from 5 seconds to 20 minutes in trained road cyclists. | Trunk training study, female mountain bikers; Sitko et al. 2024; Scand J Med Sci Sports 2016 |

## What this means for Epoch MTB

1. **Message:** MTB is a whole-body sport. "Built in the dirt" is backed by the
   data: surges, upper body, grip, skill and crash resilience.
2. **Program focus** (fitness and lifestyle coaching, Optimize):
   - Upper body and grip: pushing, pulling and carries, triceps and forearm work.
   - Trunk strength and anti-rotation work for bike control.
   - Repeated short efforts above threshold, not just steady zone 2.
   - Recovery and mobility after vibration-heavy and crash-heavy weeks.
   - Skills time on the bike counts as training.
3. **Data:** heart rate under-reports MTB effort. Use power, ride feel and
   recovery markers together, which the Epoch app already does.
4. **Road contrast:** Epoch Road (later) leans on sustained power, aero
   position and back health.
